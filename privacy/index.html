Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 27, 2026. This policy covers the game SpaceCorps 2027 (the desktop client, however you got it, and the game server it plays on) and this download site.

The short version: to run an online game we keep a small account and your game progress. We do not run ads, we do not sell data, and the game has no tracking and no third-party analytics. The game server keeps simple usage counts, described under “Usage statistics” below. The one thing that goes to a company other than our host is the one-time ticket that checks a Steam link, and only if you link a Steam account (see “The Steam link”).

What we store

  • Your account: your username, your email address and a hash of your password (never the password itself).
  • Your game: your pilot, ships, items, credits, quests, rankings, company and clan, and what your account does in the game, such as fights, trades and clan actions.
  • Chat: messages you send in game chat are sent through the server to the pilots who are meant to see them. See “Chat and reports” below for what is kept.
  • Reports and invites: a report you send about a pilot, and the record of an invite code you give or redeem. Both are described below.
  • Usage statistics: for each account and each day, whether it connected, how many times, for how many seconds and which version of the game it used; and the date of your first login and your total time played. See “Usage statistics” below.
  • Auction: what you list, sell, buy or bid on the Auction (pilots of level 5 and above), and the day you last looked at it. See “The Auction” below.
  • Settings: your game settings, your list of ignored pilots and whether you hide the Global chat are saved with your account, so they follow you to other computers.
  • Seasons: at the end of each season, your rank, points and totals, and what the season paid you. See “Seasons, wipes and rewards” below.
  • Your collection: your containers and special items, the days you collected the daily reward, and your achievements and titles. See “Containers, special items, daily rewards, achievements and titles” below.
  • Your diary: a journal of what your pilot did, kept on a separate service. See “The diary (Logbook)” below.
  • Steam link: only if you link a Steam account in the Steam version of the game. See “The Steam link” below.

What we do not collect

  • No advertising, no ad identifiers and no tracking across other sites or games.
  • No third-party analytics and no tracking across other sites or games. The game does not send details about your computer anywhere; what it sends our own server for usage counts is described under “Usage statistics”.
  • No payment data. The game has no purchases today. If that ever changes, this page will say so first.

Connections and IP addresses

Like any online service, the game server and the company hosting it (currently Sliplane) can see the IP address you connect from. We use it to run the service and keep it safe, not to profile you. The same is true for GitHub, which hosts this site and the game's downloads: when the game checks for updates or you visit the download page, GitHub sees your IP address.

The server also keeps, in memory only, how many sign-ups and failed sign-in attempts came from one network address, to stop guessing of passwords and mass sign-ups. That count is not written to the database and ends with the time window (or when the server restarts).

Chat and reports

When you write in the game's chat (Global, Local or Group), the game server receives your message and sends it to the pilots who can read it in that channel. The server also writes each chat line to its operating log, with the channel, the map, your pilot name and the text. That log is kept by our hosting provider for up to 7 days and is read when we look into a problem or a report. The game's administrators can also read its latest lines (up to 5,000, kept in the server's memory only and gone when the server restarts) on the game's Admin page, and see which pilots are connected right now (pilot name, company, world and map) while they are connected. Apart from a line quoted in a report (below), chat lines are not stored in our database and are not shown again after a server restart.

You can report a pilot from the chat: right-click one of their chat lines and choose Report. A report stores your player number and the player number of the pilot you report, the chat channel, the reason you picked (spam, abuse or harassment, cheating, or something else), the last chat line of that pilot that you saw (at most 200 characters) and the time. The server's log also gets one line about the report, with both pilot names, the channel, the reason and the quoted line. The game's administrators read the reports. The pilot you report is not told who reported them, and nobody is punished automatically because of a report.

A report is deleted 30 days after an administrator has marked it reviewed, and in any case 180 days after it was sent. If you delete your account, the reports you sent are kept without your name (they are about someone else), and the reports about your pilot are deleted with it.

Invite Friends

In Community, Invite Friends gives your pilot a personal invite code, made at random. When a friend redeems your code, the server saves who invited whom (both player numbers), the code, the time, what the starter pack gave, and the time the friend's pilot reaches level 5. That lets you see your friend's pilot name and whether they reached level 5, and collect your rewards.

When you redeem someone's code, the same record is made about you, and it also holds a keyed hash of your network address. The hash is made on our server from your address and a secret that only the server has, and cannot be turned back into your address; the address itself is not saved. It is used to limit how many codes can be redeemed from one network address in 24 hours, and the game's administrators can see its first characters next to each redemption to spot one address redeeming many codes. The hash is erased from our database 30 days after the redemption (leftover bytes in unused space of the database file can remain until that space is reused, and our hosting provider's backups age out within 7 days). The server's operating log gets a line when a code is redeemed or a reward collected, with player numbers and no address.

If you delete your account, your player number is removed from these records (the records stay, without it, so that limits and your friends' rewards still add up), and your invite code and the record of the invite rewards you collected are deleted.

Usage statistics

The game's administrators see usage statistics. They are counts: how many pilots played on a day, for how long, how many sessions, how many kills, how much in-game currency was created and spent, how many accounts were made, and how the game server was doing. Behind the daily and monthly counts the server keeps, for each account and each day, only whether the account connected, how many times, for how many seconds and which version of the game it used (for example 0.4.12 from Steam), and nothing else: no address, no name, no chat text, no position. These records are kept for 365 days and are deleted with your account. The counts themselves contain no account, name or address and are kept for 400 days. The game sends its version number and where it came from (Steam, Hangar or the website) with its requests, and no other information about your computer.

Your account also keeps the date and time of your first login and the total time your game has been connected to the game server, in flight or at the station. Both are shown on your pilot profile to every pilot who opens it, like your level, and to the game's administrators. They are deleted with your account. For pilots who played before version 0.4.12, the first login is the day of their first login after that update and the time played starts at zero then.

The Auction

The Auction is where pilots of level 5 and above sell and buy the items they earned, and where the game puts up an hourly lot for bids. To run it, the game server keeps in our database: for each listing, your player number as the seller, the item and how many, the price and its currency, the deposit you paid, when the listing was made, when it ends and how it ended (sold, cancelled, expired or returned); for each sale and each lot you win, a record with your player number and the other pilot's, the item, the amount, the price, the fee and the time; for each bid on a lot, your player number, the amount and the time; and, for each pilot, the last day they looked at the Auction (the game counts the pilots who looked in the last three days to size the hourly lots) and, if an administrator set one, a ban from the Auction with the administrator's reason.

Other pilots see your pilot name on your listings and in the result of a lot you lead or win; you see theirs in your sales, your purchases and your history. A sales record is deleted 90 days after the sale; a lot, with its bids, 30 days after it ended; a listing 30 days after it closed (an open listing stays while it is open). A ban stays until an administrator lifts it or the account is deleted. Leftover bytes of a deleted record can remain in unused space of the database file until that space is reused, and our hosting provider's backups age out within 7 days. The server's operating log names no pilot in a listing, a sale or a bid; it holds only the administrators' Auction actions (see the next section), with the administrator's name and the number of the pilot concerned. None of this is sent to a third party.

Changes administrators make to an account

Every change a game administrator makes to your account is saved as a record: credits, Thulium and Wipe Points added or set, honor, experience and level set, items added, changed or removed, a password reset, any other edit of your pilot's data, and a kick out of the game. A record holds the administrator's account number, your player number, the kind of change, the values before and after (such as a number of credits, or the name and quantity of an item) and the time. The server's operating log gets a line about it with the same facts and the two pilot names. A password is never recorded: not in the record, not in the log, and the server keeps only the scrambled form of any password. A record is deleted one year after it was made. If you delete your account, your player number is removed from the records about you. Actions on the Auction (cancelling or holding a listing, banning or unbanning a pilot from it, pausing it or voiding a lot) are recorded in the same way, with the administrator's account number, the pilot concerned and the time, and are deleted after a year too.

Seasons, wipes and rewards

At the end of each season, when the Universe Wipe runs, the game writes down what your pilot was: your rank, your place on your company's ladder, your points, your totals (level, experience, honor, credits, Thulium, kills, deaths, shots and the time you have played), how many ships the wipe kept and how many items it deleted, and what the season paid you (Wipe Points, Premium days, credits, Thulium, looks, containers and titles, including rewards that are still waiting to be paid). While a season runs, the game also keeps your totals from its start, so that it can show how you did in this season next to your whole career. It also keeps the best 50 of each ranking board as it stood at each wipe: the place, the pilot's player number, the points and the company, and no name (a board shows the pilot's current name). If the wipe fails for a pilot and has to be repeated, the game keeps a short note of it (the player number, the season, the error and the time) until it is done. Before a wipe starts, the server stores one report of what the wipe is going to do, with counts and, for the five pilots who would lose most to the caps on credits and Thulium, their player numbers and amounts. That report is replaced at the next wipe, and deleting an account does not remove those numbers from it before then.

All of it is kept for as long as your account exists and is deleted when you delete your account. The ranking boards of past seasons keep their places, points and companies, but without your player number: your place then shows as “Deleted pilot”. Other pilots see, on your profile, your rank, your places and your level at each past wipe and your kills in the current wipe, and the boards of past seasons show a pilot's name, company and points. The rest (your deaths, shots, distance and time played in a wipe, the honor you lost) is shown to you and to the game's administrators only.

Containers, special items, daily rewards, achievements and titles

Containers. When you open a container, the game server records which container it was, the prize, the random number it drew (with the seed it used and the version of the table of chances), your guarantee counter and the time. It also keeps how many containers you hold and how each one reached you (for example, given by an administrator, with the administrator's account number, or a reward). The record of each opening is kept for one year and then deleted; the rest is kept as long as your account. Everything is deleted when you delete your account. The chances are published on the wiki page “Containers”.

Special items. The game keeps the special items you hold (how many of each kind), a record of each time one was given to you, used or taken away (which kind, when, what it was used on and, for an item an administrator gave or took, the account number of the administrator and the note he wrote), and the special items that are working for you now (which one and when it ends). All of it is kept as long as your account and is deleted when you delete your account.

Daily rewards. The game keeps a record of the days on which you collected the daily reward: the season, which tile, chest or prize step it was, the day, whether you collected it late, whether you or an administrator collected it, the time and what it gave you. It is kept for as long as your account exists and is deleted when you delete your account.

Achievements and titles. The server keeps which achievements you have unlocked and when, the lifetime counters behind them (for example how many aliens and pilots you destroyed, how many hours you played, the most credits or Thulium you have held, the highest level you reached, how many wipes you have been through, and whether you have died or are in a clan), the rewards an achievement still owes you, a note of an achievement an administrator removed from you (so that it pays only once), the titles you own and the one you wear. Other pilots can see the title you wear (on your profile, in the rankings and next to your name in some windows) and, on your profile, how many achievements you have and your three rarest. All of it is deleted when you delete your account.

The diary (Logbook)

The game keeps a diary of what your pilot did. An entry is a short line: when it happened and what it was (you reached a level or a rank, bought, crafted or sold something, destroyed aliens or another pilot or were destroyed, mined asteroids, joined or left a clan, finished a mission or a research, opened a container, collected a daily reward, used a special item, redeemed a bonus code or an invite, earned an achievement or a title, linked a Steam account, were wiped and what the wipe paid you). Alien kills and mining are folded into one line for each hour. Routine entries are kept for 21 days and milestones, such as a new level, a finished mission or a wipe, for a year; at most the newest 400 routine and 500 milestone entries are kept.

Only you see your diary, and the game's administrators, who can read it for support: each time an administrator reads it, that is recorded like the other things administrators do (see “Changes administrators make to an account”). The diary is kept by a small separate service that we run at the same hosting provider as the game's database, on a volume of its own. A line holds your player number, the time, the kind of entry and the game names it is about (an item, an alien, a sector, a clan's name), with amounts of in-game currency. It holds no chat, no account name, no email address and no network address. When another pilot is in a line (the pilot who destroyed you, or whom you destroyed), the line holds only that pilot's player number, and the page shows the name when you read it.

If you delete your account, your diary is deleted from the service: the game server tells the service right after the deletion and keeps trying until it answers. Copies in our hosting provider's backups age out within 7 days, as for the game's database.

The Steam link

If you link your Steam account (Settings › Account, in the Steam version of the game), the game server stores your Steam ID (a number Steam gives your account) and the time you linked it, and nothing else from Steam: not your Steam name, friends, games or password. To check the link, the game sends the server a one-time ticket from Steam, and the server sends that ticket to Valve, the company behind Steam, which tells the server whether the ticket is genuine and whose Steam ID it belongs to. Valve receives the ticket, the game's Steam app number and our server's key, and sees our server's address; we send it nothing else about you, not your pilot name or account number. The link tells the game which Steam account may receive this pilot's achievements, which the game on your own computer then reports to Steam (only when the Steam account you are signed in to is the one you linked). Unlinking in Settings, or deleting your account, deletes the stored Steam ID at once. For 24 hours after you unlink, the server also keeps the time you unlinked and a scrambled code made from that Steam ID with a secret that only the server holds, which it uses only to tell whether you link the same Steam account again; both are then deleted. Administrators can see that a pilot is linked and which Steam ID it is, and can remove or move the link. Other pilots never see your Steam ID. What Steam itself holds is covered by Valve's privacy policy.

Crash reports

If the game crashes, it saves a report on your computer (in ~/.spacecorps2027/crash-reports/). Sending it is off by default and happens only if you turn on Settings › General › Send crash reports. A sent report becomes a public issue on the SpaceCorps repository on GitHub, so it is visible to everyone. It describes the crash, the game version and your operating system, not your password.

On your computer

The game keeps your settings, window layout and sign-in on your computer, in the ~/.spacecorps2027/ folder (in your user folder on Windows and macOS). Delete the folder to remove them.

Steam and other stores

If you get the game through Steam or another store, that store handles your download, updates and purchases under its own privacy policy. To play, you still sign in with a SpaceCorps account, and this policy covers that account. In the Steam version you can also link your Steam account to your pilot; see “The Steam link”.

Who sees your data

Other pilots see your pilot name, ship, company, clan, rankings, chat messages, the date of your first login and your total time played, and (if you use the Auction) your pilot name on your listings and in the result of an Auction lot you lead or win, and the title you wear, your number of achievements and your three rarest, your rank, places and level at past wipes and your kills in the current wipe. Your email address, your password hash and your Steam ID are never shown to other players. We do not sell your data, and we do not share it except with the hosting providers above, which run the service for us, with Valve if you link a Steam account (the one-time ticket described under “The Steam link”), or when the law requires it.

Deleting your account

You can delete your account yourself in the game: Settings › Account, with your password. This removes the account and everything it owns (pilot, ships, items, and so on). If you lead a clan, it passes to its highest-ranking member, or is disbanded if nobody else is in it. Your Auction listings, your bids' link to you, the day you last looked at the Auction and any Auction ban are deleted; your player number is removed from the sales records, bids and lots, which stay without it until their 90 or 30 days are over so that the other pilot's history still adds up. Everything described above under “Seasons, wipes and rewards”, “Containers, special items, daily rewards, achievements and titles”, “The diary (Logbook)” and “The Steam link” is deleted with the account too (the ranking boards of past seasons keep their places and points without your player number). Chat lines other pilots already saw may remain on their screens, and the server's operating log keeps its lines for the 7 days described above. If you cannot sign in, ask us on Discord and we will delete it for you.

Children

SpaceCorps is not aimed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly keep accounts for them. If you think a child has an account, tell us and we will delete it.

Changes

If this policy changes, we update the date at the top and describe the change in the release notes.

Contact

Questions, requests to see or delete your data, or anything else about privacy: our Discord or an issue on GitHub. Please do not post passwords or other private details in public.
