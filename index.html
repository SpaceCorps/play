SpaceCorps 2027

Fly for a corporation, hunt aliens with your clan and climb the season ranking. A native game for macOS, Windows and Linux.

Real frames from the client, rendered by its own engine.

What's in it

The SpaceCorps MMO, rebuilt as a native desktop game.

  • Painted ships

    Protos, Paragon, Wraith and Ostirion carry their own liveries, panel lines, hull numbers and blinking navigation lights.

  • Real-time combat

    Lasers, rockets, shields and drones against five alien species and other pilots, simulated by an authoritative server.

  • Three corporations

    Fly for Mars Colonization, Terra Space Group or Galactic Ventures, and push into the PvP sectors at the heart of the map.

  • Clans

    Found or join a clan: members and ranks, a shared bank, clan levels, and diplomacy from treaties to war.

  • Seasons

    Thirty days from a fresh start to the Blackhole Eruption. Wipe points buy permanent boosts that outlast the reset.

  • Galaxy Gates

    Materialize the 100 parts of the Chrono-Gate to open the Transport Cache and pick next season's sector.

  • Crafting

    Craft and fuse equipment in the Assembly, and run Skylab station modules that produce resources.

  • Ten languages

    English, Deutsch, Español, Français, Português, Svenska, Русский, 日本語, 한국어 and 简体中文.

What's new

The latest update. The patch notes are written in English.

0.5.2

8 November 2026 · GitHub

SpaceCorps 2027 0.5.2 rebalances the ships: every base ship has new hull, speed and laser numbers, the thirteen designs follow their ships, every ship has an auxiliary slot, the Helios Beam hits softer, and three design abilities are stronger (Venom, Heal Pod, Blink). The Wraith gets a tenth hull plate slot with a technology of its own. The Clan Gates become Clan Dungeons, and their boss room opens when 75 % of the aliens are destroyed. It adds keys and screens that are easier to learn (Esc closes windows, a shorter sidebar), a skippable tour for new pilots, Auction lots for each world, and the first build of flying from other servers, which stays switched off until it is opened.

Everything new in 0.5.2

Highlights

  • Storm: hull 180,000 (150,000), speed 260 (250), 14 lasers (13).
  • Ironclad: speed 110 (92), 8 lasers (6).
  • Wraith: hull 256,000 (324,000), speed 210 (220), 13 lasers (12), 10 hull plate slots (9).
  • Paragon: speed 230 (210), 9 lasers (8).
  • Ostirion: hull 64,000 (48,000), speed 220 (200). Nomad: hull 111,000 (96,000), speed 210 (200). Protos: speed 180 (160). Kitefin: speed 190 (175).
  • Every ship has an auxiliary slot: the Protos, the Kitefin and the Ostirion get one (they had none).
  • The designs follow their ships: a design is its ship plus its own changes, so every Storm design has the Storm's new hull, speed and laser slot, and so on (the table below).
  • Helios Beam: 150 damage (185), crit chance 20 % (25 %).
  • Venom 125,000 (100,000). Heal Pod twice as strong: 20,000 + 2 % of the healed ship's hull a second (10,000 + 1 %). The BRATAN gets the Heal Pod like the MENATI. TITANIC's Blink is not capped any more: 2,500 like the NOTSUM's.
  • The Clan Gates are now Clan Dungeons (the words need the new game; the server's messages change at once). The toolbar button and window, the Dungeons tab, the clan portal, the call card with Join, the notices, the Help cards, the wiki page and the aliens' names ("Dungeon Goombah") all say dungeon in the 13 languages. Nothing else changes in this item: the same maze, fog, pay, limits (95 / 130 / 165 minutes) and rules.
  • The Warlord's room opens when 75 % of the aliens are destroyed. Before, the Crystalys and their Twins (about 45 aliens) had to die and the rest of the maze could be left alone. Now the party destroys three quarters of the aliens the dungeon was made with, rounded up: 177 of the 235 aliens of a party of 5 (the wiki's table of the aliens gives the count for a party of 5). The aliens that count are the ones in the packs of the maze, the packs that stand in the corridors from the first second among them, whoever destroys them. The waves of the loot zones, the Goombahs the Warlord calls and the Warlord itself do not count. The objective line reads "Destroy 75 % of the aliens to unlock the boss room (N left)", and the chip, the minimap and the wiki say the same. Once the room is open it stays open.
  • Auction lots for each world: Alpha, Beta and Gamma each get their own hourly lots, and you bid on the lots of your own world. The Market stays shared (below).
  • Keys and screens (needs the new game): Esc closes windows; Enter and Ctrl/Cmd+Enter answer dialogs and the dialogs show their keys; a sidebar of five pages and a More row; a Next-step card on the Dashboard; a ? Windows guide on the flight toolbar; big windows cascade.
  • A skippable tour for new pilots (needs the new game): 11 steps in the station, 17 in your first flight, skippable with one confirmation and replayable from Settings > General.
  • Flying from other servers, built and switched off: the game can list regional servers (Singapore, US East) and let you fly from one. None is registered at release, so nothing changes for you yet.
  • ARM64 downloads, ready and switched off: the release job and the updater know Linux ARM64 and Windows on Arm packages; this release ships the four packages of 0.5.1.

If you already play: what changes for you

  • The numbers are the server's. Ship hulls, speeds, slots, the Helios Beam and the abilities reach every game at once, with the server. A 0.5.1 game shows them (it reads the server's numbers) but its help cards and design descriptions keep the old words until you update the game.
  • Your ships keep their share of hull. A one-time data step (hulls-v3) multiplies the saved hull of every Storm, Ostirion and Nomad and of the NOTSUM, BRATAN and RECON by the growth of its hull, so a full ship is still full and a damaged one keeps its share. The Wraith and its five designs lose hull: a saved hull above the new maximum is cut to it when the ship launches (no step needed).
  • Nobody is refunded or compensated. Items, plates, Credits and Thulium are untouched.
  • A 0.5.1 game keeps working with the 0.5.2 server. The old game still says "gate" in its own windows until you update, shows the server's new dungeon messages in English only, and counts the room's "left" as the kills still needed, so its objective line is right for the new rule except for the word "guards".
  • A Dungeon that is open when the server updates ends with the restart, as for any restart; its price goes back to the clan bank.
  • Your board is your world's. The Lots page shows the lots of the world you fly in when you open it. If you choose another world after a wipe, you see that world's lots.
  • A lot you lead stays with you. In the rare case that your world changes while you lead a lot (a world is chosen at Setup and again for the next season, and the wipe voids the open lots and refunds the leading bids), the lot stays on your list with a small world capsule, you are refunded at once if you are outbid, and you get the goods when it ends. The "In bids" capsule counts the lots you lead in every world, and your results show the lots you won in any world.
  • Lots that are open when the server updates were made by 0.5.1 and belong to no world: every world sees them and bids on them until they end (4 hours at most, up to 10 minutes more with the soft close). The worlds' own lots begin with the next UTC hour; an hour that 0.5.1 already made lots for is left as it is.
  • The Market is as it was. Listings and buy orders reach pilots of all three worlds, and the Market's prices are not split.
  • Smaller lots in a thin world. In a world with few active pilots the lots are small (down to a tenth of the table); in a busy one they are full size. In the model's run with 17 pilots who look at the Auction, split 10 / 4 / 3 across the worlds, the goods on offer in total hardly change (151.6 M against 152.7 M a day). How many pilots look at the Auction in each world today is not measured.
  • A 0.5.1 game keeps working. It shows the board the server sends (its own world's), has no world line, and shows the refusal for another world's lot in English.
  • ARM64: nothing changes for you while the switch is off. There is no ARM64 download unless the owner switches it on for the release. A Windows x64 game that runs under emulation on a Windows on Arm PC reports itself as x86_64 and keeps updating from the x64 zip.
  • At release nothing changes for you. No regional server exists yet, so the start card and the Launch sheet look as in 0.5.1 and you launch from Helsinki as ever. The only difference is in the background: while the start card is on screen, the 0.5.2 game asks Helsinki for the server list every 5 seconds (a small request, answered "not changed" when the list is the same) and measures its ping to each listed server every 20 seconds. With Helsinki alone in the list it shows nothing.
  • A 0.5.1 game never asks for the list, flies from Helsinki exactly as before, and does not see regional servers even once they are public. Update to 0.5.2 to use them. A 0.5.2 game on a server with no list (an older server) is played as before and does not ask again.
  • While the regional servers are staff only (the soak of 3 to 5 days the plan calls for), only admin accounts see and launch them, marked "Staff only". For every other pilot the list stays hidden.
  • Once the regional servers are public (a later step the owner takes; not part of this release): your first launch is still from Helsinki. Pick another row in the Launch sheet to fly from it; the game remembers the pick for that pilot on that PC until you pick another. Your ship, items, Credits, Thulium, clan and rank are the same pilot's, wherever you fly.
  • Plan for the station. If you want the Shop, Assembly, Skylab, Auction, clan actions or a mission claim, dock first. See "What does not count" above for achievements, the Clan Dungeons and Davidski's pay on a regional server.
  • This part moves nothing. The server list and the flights add no data step and touch no pilot row; no Credits, Thulium or items move because of them, and no pilot changes anything until they pick another server. (The release's own data step hulls-v3, which scales the saved hull of some ships, is separate and not part of this lane.)
  • Update the game to see any of this. The server alone changes nothing you can see here, except that a new account's kit holds a Damage Amp I. The update moves no Credits, Thulium or items.
  • A 0.5.1 game keeps working with the 0.5.2 server. It has none of the keys, screens or the tour. When it saves its settings it merges them into the stored ones, so it keeps the new settings keys it does not know (tutorial, sidebarMore, keyBindingsMigrated).
  • Esc in flight. If you keep Quests, Log or Pilot docked and press Esc to drop a target: a panel that was open when the flight began stays. A panel you opened by hand in this flight closes on that Esc, and the target is cleared by the next one.
  • Dialogs that spend need two Enters, or Ctrl/Cmd+Enter. Dialogs that delete answer Enter with Cancel. Launch and Return to Base confirm on one Enter. Nothing answers in the first 0.4 s after a dialog appears.
  • Key bindings. You can no longer bind Esc or Ctrl+Enter. If one of your bindings used either, it goes back to its default once, with one notice.
  • Sidebar. From level 2 you see every page as before (the open choice is yours to change with the More row). A level 1 pilot sees five pages and More, and one click on More is remembered. A replay of the tour folds the sidebar again until it ends, unless you saved a choice.
  • The tour. You get it only if you are still in Setup or at level 1 with under 1,000 experience. Any other pilot gets nothing and can start it from Settings > General. A replay costs and gives nothing.
  • Changed words. Look for the new names: Engage is Launch, Star System is Galaxy Map, Galaxy Chat is Chat, Stay and Keep it are Cancel, Connect Neural Link is Join this world, Enlist in {tag} is Join {tag}. Window ids, saved window layouts and key bindings are untouched.
  • Windows under 1280 points wide keep the 0.5.1 toolbar (no dividers, no Windows guide).
  • Nothing is taken away. No page is locked, no window is removed, no Credits, Thulium or item moves.

Items and abilities

  • Helios Beam: damage 150 (185) and the laser's own crit chance 20 % (25 %). Crit Amp III and IV keep their +25 points. A plain volley of x1 ammo averages about 148.5 (187.3), so the Expedition's magazines, which are made from the Helios volley, follow.
  • Venom (RAPTOR): 125,000 hull damage over 30 seconds (100,000), the same cooldown. A Venom ticks 600 times of 0.05 s.
  • Heal Pod (MENATI, and now BRATAN): 20,000 + 2 % of the maximum hull of each ship it heals, every second for 5 seconds (10,000 + 1 %), radius 600, cooldown 120 s. It heals the caster, the group and the company mates, as before. In its five seconds it restores 88 % of a bare Paragon's hull.
  • Blink (NOTSUM, TITANIC): the TITANIC's Blink has no cap on its speed any more: 2,500 for 1 second, the NOTSUM's. Nine of the thirteen designs have an ability (eight).
  • Laser ammo costs a tenth of the Cataclysite and Quorvium (owner, 2026-11-07: these are rare resources, players take hours to farm that much, and 1,000 rounds are less than a minute of shooting). A batch of 1,000 now asks: Ultra Core (x3) 120 Cataclysite and 90 Quorvium (1,200 and 900), 687 Thulium (568), 320 Credits (360); Experimental Fusion Core (x4) 140 and 105 (1,400 and 1,050), 1,315 Thulium (1,176), 9,058 Credits (9,138); Dark Matter Ammo I 120 and 90 (1,200 and 900), 987 Thulium (868), 4,873 Credits (4,913). The plates, the Dark Matter, the craft times and the 1,000 per batch are unchanged. What a unit is worth does not move: the freed worth is paid in Thulium at 200 Credits each, so the Market's prices, the Auction's reserve and Davidski's offers are the same as before.
  • The Auto-Rocket CPU fires at asteroids too (owner, 2026-11-07: "auto rocket cpu should work on asteroids too"). Until 0.5.1 an order of your lasers on an asteroid was no fight for it; now it is. The rules are the ones it follows at a ship: it is ON only when you pressed it, it fires your armed rocket whenever the Fire Rocket key would work (the shared timer, a rocket left, the rock within the rocket's reach), a rocket fired by hand always works, and with no key, click or mouse move for 3 minutes it waits (a parked ship spends at most 60 rockets). A guided rocket is named for the rock and an unguided one flies at its centre. It spends rockets on stone as it does on a ship, so switch it OFF while you only mine. The Help card and the wiki say so in 13 languages.
  • The Tesseract's Facets fly twice as far from it (owner, 2026-11-07: "facets should fly 2x the distance away from tesseract as they do now"). They roam a disc of 3,600 units round the Tesseract (1,800), a new leg every 3 seconds as before. Spread over four times the area, fewer of the hundred are in range of any group: for sixteen Level 8 Paragons spread round the Tesseract 42 of the hundred (85), for the same sixteen in one place 9 (18). The Tesseract fight needs about a third fewer pilots: on x4 ammo the smallest group that wins, spread round it, is 7, 11 and 15 in Alpha, Beta and Gamma (10, 15, 20) and 3, 5 and 6 all in one place (5, 7, 9). Sixteen Level 8 Paragons still destroy it in about a minute with at most six ships lost. The Tesseract's home moved about 1,500 units toward the middle of Danger Sector 4 (from 27,000 / 5,000 to 25,900 / 6,100) so that the Facets' whole roaming disc lies on the map; it is the nearest place on the sector where the home keeps its distances from the gates, the Dormant Swamp and the black hole and the roaming disc stays on the map. The Facets still never reach a gate ring (at least 4,376 short of the nearest) or the black hole's steering circle, and a Facet's leg is never laid off the map.

Slots and research

  • Auxiliary slots: every ship has one or more now. They count as half a generator slot, as before.
  • The Ataraxis' support slot: 5 support slots (4), 13 generator slots (12). The slot that was the first auxiliary one is a support slot now and counts for 75 % instead of 50 %.
  • The Wraith's tenth hull plate slot: a new technology, Wraith Hull Plate Slot X: 1 hour of research and 10 Dark Matter, behind the ninth slot. There are 37 hull plate slot technologies and 159 technologies in all (158).
  • The Dormant Knowledge gate does not move: the new slot does not count as knowledge, so the five shares stay 30 / 59 / 88 / 117 / 146 of 146 technologies. Schematics can finish it (143 of the 159 technologies are 24 hours or less).

What follows from the numbers (nobody ordered these)

  • Conversion prices. The Dark Matter Plates a design asks follow the design's power as the model counts it: NOTSUM 25 (15), BRATAN 20 (15), ATARAXIS 35 (30), MATT 25 (20); the other nine designs ask what they asked. Thulium, fragments, plates, cores and time are the same.
  • The Ostirion is faster than the Nomad (220 against 210), and both have the Dual-Core Fusion drive on the wiki's ship page (a base speed over 200).
  • The Paragon rates as a Class III Starfighter in the client (9 lasers).
  • Davidski's TITANIC has 10 lasers (8 before) and the TITANIC's new base speed, so his volley is 4,324 a second in Alpha (4,104) and his speed 98 (89); his hull, shield and pay are what they were.
  • The Company Pilots' Ostirion has 64,000 hull and flies at 222 (48,000 and 202). Their squads hunt the Phantasm in Gamma now; they hunted it in Alpha and Beta only.
  • The swarm leaders follow the ships they are copies of (the swarm model prices them again from their toughness): the Pirate Boss (an Ironclad) flies at 110 (92) with a volley of 260 (195), its shield and pay unchanged; the Pirate Scout (a Kitefin) flies at 190 (175) with 26,364 shield (24,308) that recharges 1,756 a second (1,619). The Dormant Force (a Wraith) has 256,000 hull (324,000), speed 210 (220), a volley of 3,120 (2,880) and pays 185,000 credits, 485 Thulium, 29,200 experience and 127 honor (200,000, 535, 32,100, 139). The Dormant Pulse (a Paragon) flies at 230 (210), has a volley of 4,320 (3,840) and pays 105,000, 275, 16,600 and 72 (95,000, 255, 15,200, 66).
  • The Gate's Pirate is a copy of the Pirate Boss and flies at 110 (92): a pack of Pirates reaches the party sooner, every other Gate number is the same.
  • The Expedition's magazines hold 1,600 / 4,500 / 17,000 / 28,000 / 42,000 rounds from level 1 to 5 (1,300 / 3,600 / 14,000 / 23,000 / 35,000): the Helios volley is 150 and not 185, so a magazine lasts as many kills as before.
  • The quests' rewards do not move. The rate card the Challenge line is priced from keeps the old pay of the two Dormant leaders.

The Auction: lots for each world

  • Every world has its own hourly Auction lots (server; the new game names the world). Alpha, Beta and Gamma each get a board of their own: three lots at every UTC hour, each open 4 hours, so 12 are open at once on a board. The Market (the pilots' listings, the buy orders, Davidski's stock) is still shared by all three worlds.
  • Other goods in each world at the same hour. A world's board shows the day table's hour shifted by a fixed number of hours: Alpha 0 (the table exactly as 0.5.1 ran it), Beta 4 hours ahead, Gamma 11 hours ahead. At 08:00 UTC Alpha has Ember I, Advanced Plasma and an EMP Charge, Beta has Advanced Plasma, Ultra Core and Scatter II, and Gamma has Ember II, Advanced Plasma and an EMP Charge. In no hour do two worlds show the same three goods (a server test checks the file), and each world's day holds the table's 72 lots once.
  • A board is sized by its own world's pilots. The rule is as before: pilots of level 5 or more who looked at the Auction in the last 3 days, 30 of them make a lot the table's full size, fewer make it smaller, down to a tenth. Now only the pilots who fly in that world count: 30 active pilots in Beta give Beta full-size lots whatever Alpha does, and a world with 3 pilots gets the smallest lots.
  • You bid on your own world's lots only. The Lots page lists the lots of the world you fly in. A bid, or a new maximum, on a lot of another world is refused: "That lot is in another world. Only the lots of your own world take your bid."
  • Nothing else about the lots is retuned. Auction.json differs from 0.5.1 only by the list of worlds: the goods and sizes of the day table, the reserve (20 % of the Shop value), the least raise (5 %, at least 100 credits), the soft close, bidding in credits, three lots an hour and the 4-hour length are as in 0.5.1.

Flying from other servers (built, switched off)

  • Choose where to fly: a server list (needs the new game). The game can list Helsinki (the server it plays on today) with Singapore and US East. A row shows the name and region (Europe, Asia, North America), a ping the game measures itself, the pilots in flight there and a status (Open, Updating, Down, Full, Maintenance, Staff only; "Update the game" when the server speaks a newer version of the flight protocol than the game, "Being updated" when it speaks an older one). The ping is a TCP connection to the server's port 443, three tries, the smallest, read again every 20 seconds while the list is shown: green under 80 ms, amber from 80 up to 150, red above 150. The list is on the start card under the server line and in the Launch sheet, where you pick the row to launch from. The pick is remembered for each pilot on that PC. A pilot who never picked, and one whose picked server is full, down or of another version, launches from Helsinki. A launch that is refused gives a message and leaves you at the station.
  • Off at release: you see nothing new. When 0.5.2 goes live no regional server is registered, so the list holds Helsinki alone. The game then draws no list and offers no choice: the start card and the Launch sheet look as in 0.5.1 and every flight is flown from Helsinki. Singapore and US East appear only after the owner has set them up (see the administrators' part), and they stay "Staff only", listed and launchable by admin accounts alone, until he makes them public in a separate step. That step is not part of the release.
  • The same pilot on every server. Helsinki keeps the pilot: account, ships, items, Credits, Thulium, clan, rank and the station. When you launch on Singapore or US East, Helsinki lends that server a copy of your flight data for the flight, under one exclusive lease (one flight per pilot, on one server at a time). The regional server reports to Helsinki every 30 seconds and at the dock; Helsinki applies each report once, so a repeated or late report is never counted twice. What the flight earns (Credits, Thulium, experience, honor, kills, loot, the ammunition used, hull and position) is applied to the same pilot in Helsinki, and level and ranking points are worked out there from the merged figures. If a regional host and its disk are lost, at most the last 30 seconds of one flight's rewards are lost; a host that restarts with its disk sends what the disk holds when it is back.
  • A flight, start to finish. "Connecting to Singapore…", then the flight with a "Flying from Singapore" badge at the top right. Return to Base shows "Syncing your flight to the station…" (the game gives the respawn and the last settings save up to 3 seconds, then the server waits up to 5 seconds for Helsinki to confirm), then the station with the rewards. If Helsinki has not confirmed, the sheet reads "Waiting for the station to receive your flight…" for up to 30 seconds; after that you are at the station and told "Your flight's rewards are on their way to the station.", and the station shows them when Helsinki has them. A flight that ends without a dock (the server was told to send flights home, you launched elsewhere, or the server lost its link to Helsinki and ended the flight) ends with "Your flight from Singapore has ended. You are back at the station."; a server that refuses your connection says "Singapore did not accept your connection. You are back at the station."
  • The station stays in Helsinki. A regional server only flies. Shop, Assembly, Skylab, Auction, clan actions, bonus codes, Hangar edits, mission claims, accounts and payments are refused there (the server answers "Available at the Station: dock to use it.", in English): dock (the dock waits up to 5 seconds for Helsinki to confirm), use the station, launch again. A regional server does take respawn, swapping the ship configuration, your settings, accepting, tracking and cancelling missions, and the reads a flight needs (Hangar, inventory, abilities, research, profiles, the map, the online list). While your ship is away Helsinki refuses your station actions too ("Your ship is on a flight from another server. Dock first."). How each in-flight window shows the station-only refusal was not checked one by one.
  • What does not count while you fly on a regional server. These are switched off there, in code: achievements, the Logbook, the clan line, the Wardens and the Clan Dungeons, containers and special items, the news feed, the Auction, Invite Friends and the login calendar. Davidski's kills pay nothing there (the kill feed tells the pilots he has paid out for the day). Rank, crown and ranking points are Helsinki's: a regional server has no ladder, and Helsinki's ladders move after it has applied the flight.
  • Each server is its own sky. Its pilots, aliens, swarms, excavators, bosses, chat, groups and PvP are its own and are not shared; you meet only the pilots flying there, and there is no chat or group across servers. Each regional server has its own Alpha, Beta and Gamma, and your world choice travels with you. The season day, the Peace Protocol and the wipe countdown are Helsinki's and reach the regional server with every beat (every 5 seconds).
  • One flight at a time, and the newest launch wins. Launching on another server, or at Helsinki, while a flight is away brings the first flight home first: its final report is applied before the new flight is granted (3.0 seconds in the proof, at most about 8). The older game is told its flight has ended.
  • Season wipe. No new flight is granted from five minutes before the wipe (the list reads Maintenance and the row's tip says a season wipe is about to start; no countdown is drawn). Flights go on until about 30 seconds before the wipe, when every flight on a regional server is sent home with its rewards. A regional server takes flights again after it has rebuilt its worlds for the new season and told Helsinki (in the proof about 10 seconds after the countdown ended).
  • Your sign-in stays in Helsinki. The game gives a regional server only a launch ticket that is valid for one minute and works once, never your token or password. The regional server makes its own token, which Helsinki does not accept, and Helsinki's token is useless there. The game sends a ticket only to an https address that ends in .sliplane.app or .spacecorps.xyz and that the list from Helsinki named.
  • The aim, and what is measured. The aim is a shorter round trip in flight for pilots far from Helsinki; the station stays in Helsinki, so its menus are as far away as before. Measured from a PC in the Helsinki area (a home line) to the real Sliplane boxes: 182 ms to Singapore and 96 ms to US East (a WebSocket echo). Not measured: the round trip from a pilot's home to Singapore or US East, so no figure is promised. Each regional server takes 60 flights at a time.
  • Texts. The list, the sheets, the badge, the messages for a flight that ended and the refusals of a launch and of Helsinki's "Dock first" line are in all 13 languages. The refusal of a station action on a regional server arrives as the server's English sentence.

  • Metric route. POST /api/player/tutorial {"step", "outcome"}, signed in, answers 204. outcome is one of reached, done, skipped (Skip this step), stuck (the skip button appeared), left (the consent skip of the whole tour), replayed; anything else is 400. step is one of the 28 ids (m01 to m11, f01 to f17, listed in docs/design/onboarding-steps.golden) or tour; anything else is counted as other. 60 calls a minute per pilot (429 above), a body of at most 512 bytes (413 above). The game sends events once a second at most, drops a refusal and never retries (a 404 from an older server ends the sending for the session).
  • Metric fun.tutorial{outcome, step} is a daily counter (group fun, entry in docs/design/admin-metrics-catalog.json), aggregate only: no pilot id, no name, no table, so no privacy-page change. Read it as: reached against done against stuck against left per step; the completion rate is tour/done over tour/reached; left is recorded under the step the pilot was on, so the consent skip rate is all left events over welcome (m01) reached. There is no Tutorial card on the Admin Funnel page yet. The existing fun.quests, eco.burned, ret.rate and acq.signups are the before-and-after comparison.

All patch notes

First launch

The builds aren't signed by Apple or Microsoft yet, so your system asks once before it opens the game.

macOS: "can't be opened" or "not verified"
  1. Open the .dmg and drag SpaceCorps 2027 onto Applications.
  2. macOS 15 Sequoia and newer: open the app once and click Done. Then open System Settings › Privacy & Security, scroll to "SpaceCorps 2027" was blocked and click Open Anyway.
  3. macOS 12 to 14: right-click (or Control-click) the app, choose Open, then Open again.
  4. Or, in Terminal:
    xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Applications/SpaceCorps 2027.app"

Windows: "Windows protected your PC"
  1. Right-click the .zip, choose Extract All, and open the SpaceCorps 2027 folder. The whole game is in spacecorps2027.exe.
  2. Start spacecorps2027.exe. If SmartScreen appears, click More info, then Run anyway.

Linux: make the AppImage executable
  1. Mark it executable, then start it (or use your file manager's Properties › Allow executing as program):
    chmod +x SpaceCorps2027-linux-x86_64.AppImage && ./SpaceCorps2027-linux-x86_64.AppImage
  2. No FUSE on your system? Run it with --appimage-extract-and-run, or take the .tar.gz: unpack it and start SpaceCorps2027/spacecorps2027.

Check a download

Compare the file's SHA-256 with the one listed above:

  • macOS: shasum -a 256 SpaceCorps2027-macos-universal.dmg
  • Windows (PowerShell): Get-FileHash SpaceCorps2027-windows-x86_64.zip
  • Linux: sha256sum -c SHA256SUMS --ignore-missing
