# Quests

Missions are the main way to level up. Mission Control hands them out: every mission comes from an officer of your own company (if you [change company](/wiki/01-General/Getting-Started.md#changing-your-company), the officers become your new company's, and the `rival x-4` tasks follow it), and each pays experience, credits, Thulium and honor when you claim it. There are **88 missions for levels 1 to 8**, eleven a level, and together they carry you from a fresh Protos to the frontline of the PvP centre. Ten **Station missions** teach you the [Skylab](/wiki/03-Mechanics/Skylab.md) and pay you for building it up, and from pilot level 3 a separate line of fifty very hard **Challenge missions** pays large rewards. Station and Challenge missions pay like the level missions: the numbers in the tables are the **base**, and your world, boosters and clan boosts multiply them ([Rewards](#rewards)).

Most missions are no longer "destroy so many of these": they are short **chains**. You destroy a few aliens, fly to a point, stay in a sector for some minutes, or find an item and drive it home to Mission Control. Some add a rule: lose no more than so many hull points, or do not die.

## In one minute

- **Take them at Mission Control.** You can run 5 level missions at once, plus 3 Station missions and 3 Challenge missions.
- **Chains.** The steps of a mission with an **In order** tag open one after the other, and only the open step counts.
- **Four kinds of step besides kills.** A **quest item** that drops only for you and that you drive to Mission Control, a **point to visit**, a **stay** of some minutes in a sector and, from season day 4, a **swarm** to hunt.
- **Conditions.** A step or a whole mission may say "hull loss at most N points" or "no death". Break it and that step, or the mission, starts again. Nothing is lost for good.
- **The Challenge line.** Fifty very hard missions in five tiers of ten, from pilot level 3. Tier I pays 9,355,000 credits, 70,125 Thulium and 435,500 XP in all, as base values: they pay like a level mission ([Rewards](#rewards)).
- **Reworked in 0.4.10.** 64 of the level missions are new versions. If you had done one, it is offered again, with its full reward ([details](#reworked-missions)).

## Mission Control



- Open **Mission Control** from the toolbar inside a station's safe zone, or click the **Mission Control** bubble above the station in your base sector (it follows the station on the map, so fly or zoom out until the station is in view). The **Normal Missions** tab lists your missions by level, the **Challenges** tab holds the [Challenge line](#challenge-line), and **Completed Missions** holds the ones waiting for you to claim their reward.
- **Accept** a mission to start it. You can run **5 missions at once**; the [Station missions](#station-missions) have 3 slots of their own and the [Challenge missions](#challenge-line) 3 more. The mission you accepted last is the one you're **tracking** (a Station mission only when you track nothing and none of your missions is one that flying can advance): it shows in the **Active Quests** window, and kills and flight count toward it first. Pick another with the dots in the window's title bar.
- A **badge** with a count sits on the Mission Control button, and on its **Normal Missions** and **Challenges** tabs while you are on another tab, as long as a mission or a Challenge can be accepted. Each new one comes with one toast and a quiet sound.
- Some missions must be done **in order**. They carry an **In order** tag, and their steps are numbered: see [Chained missions](#chained-missions).
- A mission with a quest item, a stay or a condition shows its state live in Active Quests: **Carrying** with the distance to Mission Control, the time you have stayed, the hull points you have lost (**Hull damage: 1,240 / 3,000**), **Do not die**. On the mission's card the same rules show as small pills: **Hull limit 3,000 HP**, **No death**, **In order**, and the clock of a timed mission.
- **Abandon** a mission to drop it and its progress. It is back in the list at once, and you can take it again. An item you were carrying for it is gone.
- A finished mission waits under **Completed Missions** until you **claim** it. Claim it whenever you like: the reward doesn't expire.
- Every mission can be done **once per pilot**, except the level missions that were reworked in 0.4.10, which are offered once more ([see below](#reworked-missions)). Your missions, done and in progress, survive the season's wipe.

## Levels

- Each level from 1 to 8 has **ten missions and a Special**. A level's missions open when you reach it and stay open at every level above it.
- **Take them in any order.** None of the ten waits for another. (The steps inside one mission may have an order.)
- Every level has the same mix: **three** missions with a quest item, **two** that send you to points on the map, **two** that make you stay in a sector and **three** plain hunts. A mission has one to four steps, and a hunt asks for one kind of alien or for several.
- The missions of a level pay **85% of the experience** to the next level. The aliens they have you destroy pay the rest, and at low levels quite a bit more: you usually reach the next level before a set is finished.
- **One Asteroids mission for each level.** Besides its ten and the Special, every level has a mission that asks you to break a number of [asteroids](/wiki/03-Mechanics/Asteroid-Mining.md) of kinds that live on better maps as the levels rise (the first pays 60 Rivet I; those of levels 7 and 8 open on season day 4). A break counts when it pays you a share (at least 5% of the damage the asteroid takes), with rockets or lasers. It is not one of the ten, so the Special does not wait for it, but it takes one of your 5 mission slots. Mission Control lists it after the Special, marked **Asteroids**; the tables at the end of this page do not.

## Special missions

- The eleventh mission of each level is its **Special**. It is locked until **all ten** of the level's other missions are done (claimed, or waiting for their claim), and its card says how many you have left.
- Specials pay more experience than any other mission of their level, and **items** besides: lasers, shield parts, generators, Power Cores, Ship Fragments, ammo and booster time.
- The Specials of levels 3 and 4, **Hive Breaker** and **Hive Purge**, are [swarm hunts](#swarm-missions): they open from season day 4.
- Level 1's regular missions also give a small starter item each: a Light Shield Core, a Repair Drone, a Damage Amp I, an Absorption Shield Cell I, some ammo and a stack of rockets to try each kind (Lancet I, Rivet I, Ember I and Scatter I, and Lancet II from the Special).

## Chained missions

A **chain** is a mission whose steps go in a fixed order. It carries an **In order** tag on its card and in Active Quests. Its steps are numbered: a done step is checked, the **highlighted** step is the open one, and the steps after it show a lock until it is done.

- **Only the open step counts.** Your kills, your flight and your stay time go to it and to no other. Kills you made before a step opened do not count for it.
- **Why in order?** A mission tells a small story ("clear the guards, then take the core home"), and the rules need the order: a quest item can only drop once its step is open, a stay counts from the moment it opens, and the conditions of a step (hull limit, no death) run only while that step is open.
- **Missions without the tag open all their steps at once**, in any order. **Hold the Gate** has a point to reach, a four-minute stay in one stretch and kills of 8 Seekers and 3 Phantasm, all open together, so the kills count whenever you make them while the mission runs, in the stay or out of it.
- In the tables at the end of this page an arrow ("→") joins the steps of an In order mission, and a semicolon (";") joins steps that open together.

## Quest items

A quest item is something you have to bring to Mission Control. It is not an inventory item: it cannot be traded, stored or sold, it is part of the mission. **Only you can see it.** Other pilots, your group mates too, see nothing.

| Kind | How you get it | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **A drop** | Every kill of the named alien in the named sector may drop it. The table gives the chance a kill ("15% a kill") and the kill that drops it for certain ("for certain at kill 10"). The count starts when the step opens. | Combined Operations: the Cargo Pod, from the Seekers of `x-1`. |
| **A scripted drop** | No chance: the named kill, counted from the moment the step opens, always carries it. | Nest Raid: the third Phantasm of the nest carries the Nest Core. |
| **A placed item** | It lies at a point of the sector from the moment the step opens. | Frontier Defense: the Relay Part at 12,500 / 4,800 in `x-2`. |

1. **Find it.** A drop appears where the alien died, for **120 seconds**; it blinks in the last 12. If you miss it, the next qualifying kill drops it again at once. You see the item as a cyan beacon in the world (a spinning data core in a ring, with a beam rising from it) and as a cyan diamond on the minimap.
2. **Pick it up.** Fly within **200 units** of it. A message says it is on board, and Active Quests shows **Carrying** and the distance to Mission Control.
3. **Drive it home.** Fly into the safe zone of **your own company's** Mission Control: the station of your base sector `x-1`, the ring of 1,600 units around it. A green ring on the screen and on the minimap shows it. The item is delivered at once and the step is done.

Things that stop the carry:

- **Your ship is destroyed**, by an alien or by a pilot: the item is lost, and you can get it again (a placed item is back at its point, a drop comes with the next qualifying kill). Nobody can steal it: a pilot who destroys you gets nothing from it.
- **A passed hull limit** or a **no death** rule fails the carry the same way: see [Conditions](#conditions).
- **The Jump CPU and the Base CPU refuse to work while you carry an item.** Fly home through the gates. Gates, the cloak and Shield Surge are free to use.
- If you leave the game you keep carrying when you come back. A drop that was standing on the map is gone, and the next qualifying kill drops it again.
- Items are only ever in the company sectors `x-1` to `x-4`, **never in the Danger Sectors**, and only in **your own company's**: a Galactic pilot's item lies on `G-2`, never on `M-2` or `T-2`. An alien drops your item only when it dies in your own company's sector.
- In a [group](/wiki/03-Mechanics/Groups.md) every mate who counts for the kill (see [Where it counts](#where-it-counts)) and has the mission rolls and sees their own item. Nobody can give, take or share one.

> [!TIP]
> Plan the way home before you pick the item up. From `x-3` or `x-4` it is two gate jumps to `x-1`, from `x-2` one. Mend first if your hull is low (a destroyed ship loses the item) and keep out of fights on the way: repairs do not give hull-limit points back.

## Visit and stay

**Visit a point.** A visit step names a point of a sector as two numbers, "9,000 / 4,500". They are the same numbers the title bar of the minimap shows for your own ship, so you can steer by them. Fly within **400 units** of the point and it counts at once; you do not have to stop (a tour that ends at Mission Control ends inside the station's safe zone). The point is a **gold ring** on the screen and a gold diamond on the minimap. It counts only on your own company's map, not in the same sector of another company.

**Stay in a sector.** A stay step asks for some minutes in a sector. It counts only while all three are true:

- you are on the sector the step names (for an `x-n`, your own company's map);
- you are **outside every safe zone** (a station's or a gate's ring: time in a ring does not count);
- your ship is **active**: it has flown 300 units or fired a shot in the last 30 seconds. A parked ship earns nothing.

There are two kinds. A stay that **adds up** keeps what you have: leave, die, come back, and the minutes are still there, unless the stay or its mission has a hull limit or a no-death rule: then losing your ship or passing the limit starts the stay again (see [Conditions](#conditions)). A stay **in one stretch** starts again from zero when you die, jump or leave the sector (time in a ring or standing still only pauses it), and a toast says so.

Stays and visits do not compete: every open one counts at once, whatever else you are doing. Kills are different: a kill counts for one level mission only (see [Where it counts](#where-it-counts)).

> [!NOTE]
> Visits and stays may lead into the Danger Sectors (Centre Recon, Centre Vigil and Centre Hold do), where other companies' pilots can attack you. Quest items never do.

## Swarm missions

Thirteen missions ask for the ships of the [swarms](/wiki/05-Swarms/Swarms.md). Two are level Specials: **Hive Breaker** (level 3: 3 Boss Seekers, 12 Seeker Slaves and 6 Phantasm) and **Hive Purge** (level 4: 3 Boss Seekers, 12 Seeker Slaves, 2 Bulwarks and a point to visit). Eleven are in the Challenge line: Boss Seekers and Pirate Scouts (**Hive Cull**, **Scout Cull**, **Hive Culling II**, **Scout Culling II**), Pirate Bosses (**Pirate Reckoning**, **Pirate Bane**, **Pirate Doom**, **Pirate Throne**) and the Dormant Force (**Dormant Dawn**, **Dormant Dusk**, **Dormant Dominion**).

- **They open on season day 4**, the day the swarms appear ([30-day schedule](/wiki/03-Mechanics/Wipe-Timeline.md#30-day-season-schedule)). Before it Mission Control refuses them with "This mission opens on season day 4".
- A swarm ship counts under **its own name**: a Boss Seeker or a Seeker Slave is no Seeker, a Pirate Scout is no Phantasm.
- The step names no sector, because the swarms roam: the [Seeker Swarm](/wiki/05-Swarms/Seeker-Swarm.md) in `x-1` and `x-2`, the [Pirate Swarm](/wiki/05-Swarms/Pirate-Swarm.md) in `x-2` and `x-3`, the [Dormant Swarm](/wiki/05-Swarms/Dormant-Swarm.md) in the Danger Sectors. A Boss Seeker or a Pirate Boss comes back 2 minutes after it falls.
- The Dormant missions are for a crew: there is one Dormant Swarm in each world, and a group of strong ships takes it ([how the fight goes](/wiki/05-Swarms/Dormant-Swarm.md#how-the-fight-goes)). The next tier does not wait for **Dormant Dawn** or **Dormant Dusk** ([the Challenge line](#challenge-line)).
- A Boss Seeker hits harder than a new pilot's ship can bear: read [how the fight goes](/wiki/05-Swarms/Seeker-Swarm.md#how-the-fight-goes) before you start.

## Conditions

A **condition** is an extra rule on one step or on a whole mission. There are two. The tables write them as "(hull loss at most 3,000 points)" and "(no death)"; a condition on the whole mission reads "(whole mission: ...)".

- **Hull limit.** Lose no more than so many **hull points**. The count is the hull you really lose: the points the hull bar falls by, after your shield and absorbance have taken their share. Damage to the shield does not count, black-hole radiation does, and repairs give nothing back. A killing blow always passes the limit.
- **No death.** If your ship is destroyed, the condition breaks.

**When the clock runs.** A limit on a whole mission counts from the moment you accept it (and from every restart) until the mission is done. A limit on a step counts from the moment the step opens; for a carried item, from the pick-up to the delivery. It counts every hull point you lose in that time, whoever hits you, and in a mission whose steps are open together that includes the fights of its other steps.

**What a broken condition does.** Nothing is lost for good, no Thulium is charged and the mission stays active. A message tells you ("condition broken. The attempt starts again.").

| What carries the condition | What happens when it breaks |
| :--- | :--- |
| A kill step | The kills of that step go back to 0. The other steps keep their progress. |
| A carried item | The carry fails and you fetch the item again (see [Quest items](#quest-items)). |
| A visit or a stay step | The step starts again. |
| A whole mission | Every step starts again, a carried item is lost, and the clock of a timed mission keeps running. |

Dying breaks a hull limit that is running as well as a no-death rule.

The limits are set in points, for the ship you fly at that level. A carried item's limit is 3,000 hull points on levels 1 to 3, 17,000 on levels 4 to 6 and 46,000 on levels 7 and 8; stays and tours that run longer have larger numbers. They are the same in every world, so in Beta and Gamma, where the aliens hit harder, they leave you less room. Check the mission's card before you accept. A limit may also be given as a percent of your ship's hull, and the stricter one counts when both are given; every mission of this release uses points.

## Mission examples

Eight missions from the real list, from the first hour to the Challenge line. Their numbers are in the tables at the end of this page; every pay named here is the **base** (see [Rewards](#rewards)).

- **Beacon Watch** (level 1). Fly to the point 9,000 / 4,500 in `x-1`, then stay 5 minutes in `x-1`. The whole mission is **no death**: if your ship is destroyed, you begin again at the beacon. Keep moving or shooting, or the minutes do not count. Pays 700 XP, 3,500 credits, 5 Thulium, 7 honor and a Repair Drone I.
- **Combined Operations** (level 1). Destroy 8 Seekers in `x-1`, then find the Cargo Pod: every Seeker you destroy there after that has a 15% chance to carry it, and the 10th is sure. Bring it to Mission Control with a hull loss of at most 3,000 points. Pays 1,550 XP, 8,000 credits, Advanced Plasma and Ember I.
- **Hold the Gate** (level 2, 15 minutes, no death). Four steps open at once: reach the point 12,500 / 2,500 in `x-2`, stay 4 minutes in one stretch there, and destroy 8 Seekers and 3 Phantasm. The kills count whenever you make them while the mission runs. Die, and everything starts again, but the clock does not.
- **Nest Raid** (level 3). Destroy 7 Phantasm in `x-3`, fly to the nest at 7,500 / 7,600, then destroy Phantasm until the third one drops the Nest Core: it is scripted, the third kill always carries it. Bring it home with a hull loss of at most 3,000 points and without losing your ship. Pays 4,500 XP and 36,000 credits.
- **Heavy Courier** (level 4, 40 minutes). Destroy 10 Phantasm in `x-3`, reach the points 13,400 / 5,000 and 13,600 / 1,900, then take the Manifest from a Bulwark (25% a kill, sure at the 5th) and carry it to Mission Control with a hull loss of at most 17,000 points and no death. Pays 10,500 XP, 52,000 credits and 315 Thulium.
- **Hive Breaker** (level 3 Special, from season day 4). Destroy 3 Boss Seekers and 12 Seeker Slaves, in any sector, and 6 Phantasm in `x-3`, in any order. Pays 6,800 XP, 54,500 credits, 170 Thulium, a Quantum Laser II, 10 Ship Fragments and 200 Ultra Core.
- **Grand Tour** (level 8, 25 minutes, no death). Fly to 8,000 / 4,500 in `x-4`, then to 6,000 / 4,500 in the centre, destroy 2 Goombahs in a rival company's `x-4` and fly to its heart at 8,000 / 4,500. The [ring gates](/wiki/01-General/Spacemap%20Travel.md#jump-links) and the centre both lead there. Pays 66,000 XP, 165,000 credits and 1,320 Thulium.
- **Untouched** (Challenge). Destroy 25 Phantasm in `x-3` in a row while losing no more than 39,000 hull points. Go over the limit and the count goes back to 0. Pays, at base, 44,000 XP, 855,000 credits, 6,405 Thulium, 2,370 honor, an Ancient Control Unit and 5 hours of Shield Wall Booster II.

## Station missions

- Ten missions about your [Skylab](/wiki/03-Mechanics/Skylab.md), in a **Station** section at the top of the Missions page. The first, **Light the Station**, is open from level 1 and tells you where the Skylab is: Menu, Economy, Skylab. The station menu's Skylab row pulses until you have taken it or built Solar.
- They are taken, tracked, abandoned and claimed like every mission, but they don't count in the 5: you can run **3 Station missions at once** besides your other 5. A Station mission can wait for hours of upgrade timers and never holds up a combat mission.
- Taking a Station mission doesn't change the mission you're tracking when you have one that flying can advance: the Active Quests window keeps showing your combat mission, and the Station mission is a dot in its title bar (hover it for its progress, click it to show it).
- Each opens at its own **pilot level** and after the Station missions it follows (claimed first): a locked card says which. The first three open at level 1. The last, **Core Ten**, takes the Core to level 10, the level that opens the Skylab's [Research Centre](/wiki/03-Mechanics/Research.md).
- Their tasks read your Skylab: **build** a module or **raise it to a level** (done while the module is at that level or higher; no module goes above your Core, so a Solar goal can need the Core raised first), and **collect** credits from the Credit Farm (counted from the moment you accept the mission, with the Collect Resources button). A mission whose tasks are all true at once is done: one you already meet when you accept it is done at once.
- The Credit Farm costs nothing to build, so **First Farm** pays you 3,000 Credits and 100 Thulium for building it. Collecting takes time: **Payday** asks for 1,000 Credits, about two hours of a level 1 farm (500 an hour), and **Fifty Thousand** asks for 50,000, about 33 hours of the level 3 farm that **Growing Season** leaves you (1,500 an hour).
- Solar makes only 25% of its power while it upgrades, so a station with a Thulium Farm stops its farms until the upgrade is done (see [Solar Module](/wiki/03-Mechanics/Skylab.md#solar-module)). **Brighter Panels** (Solar to level 3: 5 and 15 minutes; the small station of that mission still fits), **Growing Season** (the step to level 5 takes 45 minutes) and **Open the Supply Line** (Solar to level 6: 60 minutes) tell you how long it lasts.
- **They pay like a level mission.** The numbers in the table are the **base**: your world's bonus, boosters, Premium XP and your clan's Credits and Thulium boosts multiply them ([Rewards](#rewards)). A Station mission has no world of its own, so it pays the world you fly in when you claim it. It gives no items. Their base experience is on top of the 85% the levels' missions pay (at most 5% of a level's gap).
- Station missions don't count toward the [Wipe Points](/wiki/03-Mechanics/Wipe-Timeline.md) for missions done. They survive the wipe like every mission, and so does what you built.

## The Challenge line {#challenge-line}

From **pilot level 3**, the **Challenges** tab of Mission Control opens a second line: **fifty very hard missions in five tiers of ten**, for the pilots who want more than the levels give. Every tier asks for more than the one before, and all fifty are in the game. [The tables](#challenge-missions-table) at the end of this page list every mission and every number.

- **Ten in any order.** The ten missions of a tier are open together. **Tier II** opens when you have claimed all ten of tier I, and **tier III** when you have claimed all ten of tier II. **Tiers IV and V** open when you have claimed **nine of the ten** before them: **Dormant Dawn** (tier III) and **Dormant Dusk** (tier IV) are missions of the [Dormant Swarm](/wiki/05-Swarms/Dormant-Swarm.md), which needs a crew, so they are optional.
- **Three at once**, besides your 5 level missions and 3 Station missions.
- **They pay like a level mission.** The reward in the tables is the **base**, paid once: your world's bonus, boosters, Premium XP and your clan's Credits and Thulium boosts multiply it, as [Rewards](#rewards) explains with an example. **Honor is paid in tier I only.** They survive the wipe, and they do not count for the Wipe Points of missions.
- **Kills count twice.** A kill counts for every Challenge mission that asks for it **and** for the one level mission it counts for, so a thousand-kill mission never starves your level missions.
- **Groups.** A kill counts for the pilot who is paid for it and for every group mate within **4,000 units** of the wreck who fired a laser or a rocket in the last 15 seconds. Each pilot keeps their own count.
- **Stays need shots, except in the centre.** A stay in `x-3`, in `x-4` or in a rival's `x-4` counts only while you have fired a shot in the last 60 seconds, so a parked or circling ship earns nothing. The stays in the Danger Sectors (**Centre Watch**, **Rim Vigil**, **Last Watch**), where no alien lives, count a ship that has flown 300 units or fired in the last 30 seconds, as the stays of the level missions do.

What each tier asks, with missions from the tables:

- **Tier I, Proving Ground.** The big hunts of the home sectors and the first hard tests: **Seeker Plague** and **Phantasm Tide** (1,000 of each), **Bulwark Wall** (150 Bulwarks), **Untouched** (25 Phantasm in a row, 39,000 hull points at most), two swarm hunts from season day 4 (**Hive Cull**, **Scout Cull**), **Iron Vigil** (45 minutes in one stretch in `x-3`), **Gauntlet of the Centre** (six steps through the centre, no death), **Convoy** and **Hundred in an Hour**.

- **Tier III, The Centre.** The centre and its Goombahs: 250 Goombahs (**Goombah Scourge**), 800 Bulwarks (**Bulwark Blight**), 5,000 Phantasm (**Phantasm Ocean**), 10 Crystalys (**Crystalys Trial**), an hour in the Danger Sectors (**Centre Watch**), a tour of the centre's four corners (**Four Corners**) and a Dormant Force hunt (**Dormant Dawn**).
- **Tier IV, The Abyss.** 50 Crystalys (**Crystalys Purge**), 500 Goombahs (**Goombah Rout**), 2,000 Bulwarks (**Bulwark Sea**), 90 minutes in `DS-4` (**Rim Vigil**), two hours in a rival's `x-4` (**Rival Ground**) and 10 Dormant Force (**Dormant Dusk**).

## Where it counts

Most steps name the sector where they count, and only kills and distance flown **there** count toward them. A visit, a stay and an item name their sector the same way, but they are stricter: they count only on **your own company's** map. An item that a mission puts in `G-2` lies on `G-2` and nowhere else, and a Galactic pilot finds nothing of it on Mars' `M-2` or Terra's `T-2`. Kills and distance flown count in that sector of any company. A few kill steps name none and count in **every** sector: the swarm ships of Hive Breaker and Hive Purge, and the big hunts of the Challenge line (Seeker Plague, Phantasm Tide, Bulwark Wall, Hive Cull, Scout Cull and the big hunts of the tiers after tier I). The Station steps read your Skylab and have no sector either. The sector shows next to a step as one of your own maps:

| In the tables | Means | You see (a Mars pilot) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `x-1` … `x-4` | for kills and distance flown: that sector of any company, yours or another company's; for an item, a visit and a stay: your own company's sector only | `M-3` |
| `DS-x` | any sector of the PvP centre, the Danger Sectors `DS-1` to `DS-4` | `DS-x` |
| `rival x-4` | another company's border sector (not yours), a short hop through the ring gates or across the centre | `T-4 · G-4` |

The missions climb the sectors with your level:

| Level | Where you fight | Where it leads |
| --: | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | your base (`x-1`) and second sector (`x-2`) | – |

## Time limits

- Some missions are **timed**. Mission Control shows the limit on the mission's card before you accept it (a clock and "15 min"), and once you accept it a **countdown** runs on the card and in Active Quests. It turns amber in the last minute and red when time is up.
- A timed mission that runs out **fails**: it leaves your active missions and you can accept it again at once, from the start.
- The limits in the tables below are **Alpha's**. Beta's aliens are 1.5 times as strong and Gamma's twice, so their limits are 1.5 and 2 times as long. The clock is set when you accept the mission, for the world you're in.
- Steps have no time limit of their own: the mission's limit covers all of them. A broken condition restarts a step or the mission, but it never resets the clock.

## Rewards

- Experience, credits, Thulium and honor are multiplied by the [world](/wiki/03-Mechanics/Wipe-Timeline.md#worlds-alpha-beta-and-gamma) the mission was done in: 1x in Alpha, 2x in Beta, 3x in Gamma. Your boosters (the Experience Booster, the Honor Booster) add to them, and premium doubles the experience. The Thulium and credits boosts of your [clan](/wiki/03-Mechanics/Clans.md) add to the credits and the Thulium of every mission you claim: level, Station and Challenge.
- **Station and Challenge missions pay like level missions.** The numbers printed in their tables are the **base**. A Challenge mission is paid by the world you did it in, like a level mission (the lowest one, should it span two). A Station mission has no world of its own: it pays the world you fly in when you claim it.
- **An example.** **Seeker Plague** (Challenge) has a base of 1,100,000 credits, 8,260 Thulium, 31,500 XP and 3,060 honor. Done and claimed in Gamma with nothing switched on, it pays 3,300,000 credits, 24,780 Thulium, 94,500 XP and 9,180 honor. With your clan's Credits and Thulium boosts at their top (+10% each) it pays 3,630,000 credits and 27,258 Thulium.
- **The clan bank cap.** However big a claim is, a pilot can send at most 1,000,000 credits into [clans](/wiki/03-Mechanics/Clans.md#2-donations) in any 24 hours.
- **Items and booster time are the same in every world.** Items go to your inventory, booster hours add to what the booster has left, and ammo is ready to fire even if you claim while flying.
- **Items are Marketable.** What a mission pays in items (gear, ammo, rockets, materials, Reinforced Plates) can be sold on the [Auction](/wiki/03-Mechanics/Auction.md#marketable-items) from level 5. The Challenge missions pay no gear: their items are materials and Reinforced Plates.

## The reworked missions {#reworked-missions}

Update 0.4.10 reworked the level missions into chains. **64 of the 88 are new versions**; the 24 that stayed as they were are the plain kill missions (like Basic Training: Combat) and the Specials of levels 1, 2 and 5 to 8.

If you had **done** one of the 64, you do the new version too. At your first flight after the update a System line tells you: "Mission Control has new missions: the level quests were reworked."

- **The mission is offered again.** It is back in Mission Control, and it pays its **full reward** again: experience, credits, Thulium, honor and items, by the world you do it in.
- **A Special locks again** until the level's regular missions are done again, so level 3's ten missions come before Hive Breaker once more.
- **A reward you had not claimed yet** is paid as before (the larger of the old and the new reward), and then the new version is offered.
- **A mission you had running** is carried over to its new steps by the share you had done. A visit counts only when its share is whole, and a quest item is never handed in by that conversion.
- **Nothing goes down.** Your level, your statistics and your Wipe Points stay. A mission you do again does not count for the [Wipe Points](/wiki/03-Mechanics/Wipe-Timeline.md#earning-wipe-points) a second time: it was counted the first time.
- The Station missions, the Challenge line and the 24 missions that stayed are not touched.

## Danger

The higher sectors are where the missions pay, and where you can die. Plan your kit for them:

- **PvP**: other companies' pilots can attack you in `x-4` and the centre in every world, from `x-2` up in Beta, and everywhere in Gamma (except in safe zones and during the Peace Protocol). The ring gates bring other companies' pilots into your `x-3` too: in Beta and Gamma one of them can destroy you there while you carry an item.
- **Bulwarks** (`x-3`, `x-4`) outrun a Protos and hit hard. Fight them from an [Ostirion](/wiki/02-Ships/Ostirion.md), near a gate's safe zone, and pull back into it to repair.
- **Goombahs** (`x-3`, `x-4`) have a range of 800, hit for 3,000 and never start a fight: leave one alone and it leaves you alone, shoot it and it turns on you until 10 seconds after your last hit (and it mends when left alone). A ship whose lasers are all [Starfire-III](/wiki/06-Items/Lasers.md) (range 850) outranges them and Bulwarks alike: with a ship faster than they are (an Ostirion or above; a Goombah flies 180, a Bulwark 175) you can fire without taking a hit. Your ship's range is the average of its lasers' ranges, so a Starfire-III beside two Quantum Laser II fires from 750: far enough to keep away from a Bulwark (700), not from a Goombah (800). Level 5's Special gives you one; you can also make one in Assembly, from Velkonite Reinforced Plates that the Forgery of your [Skylab](/wiki/03-Mechanics/Skylab.md) makes.
- **Crystalys** roam the border (`x-4`). Nothing in reach of level 8 outranges one, and only a fast ship outruns it: keep your distance, and watch the minimap.
- When you are destroyed you choose where to come back: at your base, at the nearest portal or on the spot (see [Dying and Coming Back](/wiki/01-General/Getting-Started.md)). A timed mission's clock keeps running meanwhile, a carried item is lost, and a no-death rule breaks.
