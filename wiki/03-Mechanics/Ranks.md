# Ranks

Every pilot has a **rank**, from Junior Pilot to Senior Admiral. It is a military ladder of 8 tiers with 3 grades each, 24 ranks in all. Your rank is your **place among the pilots of your company**, counted in **PvE points**: the points you earn from your level, your experience and the aliens you destroy. In flight the game shows only a small **symbol** before a pilot's name, so you can tell the best pilots of a company from a fresh one at a glance. The words are on the Company page and in the hover tip of a symbol.

**In one minute**

- Your company keeps one list of its pilots, the pilot with the most PvE points first. Your rank comes from your place in that list.
- Only the best pilot of the company is the **Senior Admiral**. Everybody else is cut from the top down into the other 23 ranks: few pilots near the top, most at the bottom.
- Every rank also asks for a **minimum of PvE points**, so a small or new company cannot hand out the top ranks for nothing. The Senior Admiral needs 20,000.
- Your rank moves. It falls when other pilots pass you and rises when you pass them. A fall is never announced; a new best rank is.
- Only pilots with PvE points who flew in the last 30 days are on the list. Everybody else is a Junior Pilot until they fly again.
- The wipe does not change anybody's points, so the list does not start over.
- The symbol before a name is the rank: a shape for the tier, one to three marks for the grade.

## The company ladder

The pilots of your company, **all three worlds together**, stand in one line, the pilot with the most PvE points first. When two pilots have the same points, the one with the higher level is ahead, then the older pilot. This line is the **ladder**, and your rank comes from two things:

1. **Your place.** The pilot in place 1 is the **Senior Admiral**, alone. The pilots behind him are cut into the other 23 ranks by shares of the ladder, and every rank holds a quarter more pilots than the rank above it. So about a fifth of the pilots are Junior Pilots, and the best tenth are Captains or better.
2. **Your points.** Every rank asks for a minimum of PvE points ([the table below](#the-24-ranks)). You hold the lower of the two ranks: the one your place gives and the one your points reach.

**An example.** A company has 31 pilots on its ladder. By place alone, the ranks go like this; the last column is the least PvE points the rank asks for.

| Place | Rank | Minimum PvE points |
| :---: | :--- | ---: |
| 1 | Senior Admiral | 20,000 |
| 2 | Senior Major | 4,700 |
| 3 | Senior Captain | 3,000 |
| 4 | Captain | 2,600 |
| 5 | Senior Lieutenant | 1,900 |
| 6–7 | Lieutenant | 1,500 |
| 8 | Junior Lieutenant | 1,300 |
| 9–10 | Senior Sergeant | 1,000 |
| 11–13 | Sergeant | 800 |
| 14–16 | Junior Sergeant | 610 |
| 17–20 | Senior Pilot | 350 |
| 21–24 | Pilot | 150 |
| 25–31 | Junior Pilot | 0 |

You are in place 16 of 31 with 1,800 PvE points. The Company page says "Place 16 of 31" and a small tag "Top 52%": you are among the best 52 percent of your company. Places 14 to 16 are Junior Sergeants, so that is your rank, although your points alone would reach Lieutenant (1,500). To become a Sergeant you must reach place 13. It belongs to Sable, who has 2,100 PvE points. To pass him you need 2,101, which is 301 more, and the Company page tells you exactly that: "301 PvE points to pass Sable and reach Sergeant".

The points matter at the top, too. If the best pilot of this company has 12,000 PvE points, he holds Senior General until he has 20,000, whatever place he is in.

> [!NOTE]
> The ranks are cut in whole pilots, so a small company has nobody in some of the higher ranks. A Colonel needs a company of at least 46 pilots on the ladder, a General 118, a Senior General 178, a Junior Admiral 301 and an Admiral 675. Place 1 is the exception: every company has one, and its pilot is the Senior Admiral once he has 20,000 PvE points.

## Who is on the ladder

A pilot is on the ladder of his company when **all three** are true:

- he is in a company;
- he has PvE points: the first kill puts a new pilot on the ladder;
- he has flown in the last **30 days**.

A pilot who is not on the ladder is a **Junior Pilot** wherever a rank shows, has no place and is not in the company's list. The rule is there so that a pilot who tried the game once and left does not sit at the bottom for ever, and so that a pilot who is away cannot keep the best rank for months.

> [!TIP]
> Nothing is lost while you are away. Your points are kept, and when you launch again, or just open the Company page, you are placed again with the points you left with. If you were the best pilot you are the Senior Admiral again, and the pilot who held the rank moves down one place.

If you change company, your points go with you and you stand on the ladder of the new company, in the place they give you there.

## Your rank moves

- It **falls** when other pilots pass you. Each pilot who passes you takes one place from you, and when that place was the last of your rank, you drop a grade.
- It **rises** when you pass others, as soon as your kill is counted.
- It can also move when pilots join or leave the ladder, because every rank is a share of the company. A company that grows never lowers a pilot who keeps his place; when pilots leave, a pilot at the edge of a rank can lose a grade.
- Your own rank is told to you when it **rises**, and only then ([Promotions](#promotions)). A fall is never announced: your symbol just changes.

## How you earn PvE points

Your PvE points are worked out from three things:

**level × 100 + experience ÷ 1,000 (rounded down) + the points of every alien you destroyed**

A tougher alien is worth more: a kill adds points by how much damage it takes to destroy the alien. The swarm ships and the Clan Wardens count by the same rule, so a Pirate Scout is worth as much as a Bulwark. Every kill is counted under the alien's own name in your kill statistics.

| Phantasm | 2 |

| Boss Seeker | 5 |

| Pirate Boss | 15 |
| Pirate Scout | 4 |
| Dormant Force | 25 |

| Brood Warden I, II, III | 14, 18, 35 |
| Siege Warden I, II, III | 13, 17, 32 |

| Brood Drone I, II, III | 1, 1, 2 |

| Wrath Guard I, II, III | 2, 3, 6 |

The first five rows are the regular aliens and the next six the ships of the three swarms. Last come the Clan Wardens, the leaders of a Clan Warden fight, and their crews, the Brood Drones, Siege Escorts and Wrath Guards: I, II and III are the Warden's strength, and the points of each are listed in that order.

For example, a pilot at level 4 with 12,500 experience who has destroyed 300 Seekers, 80 Phantasms and 10 Bulwarks has 400 + 12 + 300 + 160 + 40 = **912** points. That is enough for the minimum of a Sergeant (800); whether he is one depends on his place in the company.

The **Rankings** page (Community › Rankings) shows how your own points are made up, and the **Hall of Fame** on it lists the best pilots. The **(i)** next to the PvE points there lists the five aliens, what a kill in each of the swarms ([Swarms](/wiki/05-Swarms/Swarms.md)) is worth, from its smallest ship to its boss, and the [Clan Wardens](/wiki/03-Mechanics/Clans.md#clan-wardens).

## The symbols

The game draws a rank as a small symbol: a **shape** for the tier, in a colour of its own, and **one, two or three marks** for the grade: one for a Junior, two for the plain rank, three for a Senior. The colours run from cool and plain to warm and bright as the tiers rise.

| Tier | Shape | Colour |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Pilot | dots | steel blue |
| Sergeant | chevrons, one above the other | copper |
| Lieutenant | flat bars, one above the other | silver |
| Captain | diamonds | green |
| Major | upward triangles (pennants) | cyan |
| Colonel | hollow rings | gold |
| General | five-pointed stars | orange |
| Admiral | a laurel wreath open at the top, round one to three stars | rose |

## Where you see them

In flight you see **only the symbol**, never the words. It stands before the pilot's name:

- over a ship, in the name tag, and in the Target window;
- in the chat, before the name of the pilot who wrote the line;
- in your group's list, and in the hover tip of a group mate on the minimap;
- in the kill feed, before each pilot who is named (not before aliens);
- in the Hall of Fame, in the Ranking window and, large, on a pilot's profile.

The **words** (Junior Sergeant, Captain …) are on the Company page and in a hover tip: rest the pointer on a symbol. A symbol shows where a pilot stands among **his own company's** pilots, so the Major of another company is a Major among them, not among yours. [Company pilots](/wiki/03-Mechanics/Company-Pilots.md) are the game's own squads and have no rank.

## The company ranking

Open **Economy › Company**. Its **Company ranking** section shows, from the top:

- **Your rank**: its symbol and name, your place ("Place 16 of 31") and a small tag with your percentile ("Top 52%").
- **A bar and a line under it** that say what the next rank takes. A line like "301 PvE points to pass Sable and reach Sergeant" names the last pilot of the next rank: pass him and the rank is yours. When only your points hold you back, the line says how many more PvE points you need. The Senior Admiral reads "The highest rank."
- **The best pilot** of your company, with his points.
- **All ranks**, shut until you open it: the 24 ranks as a pyramid, each with a bar as wide as its share of the company, its **Share**, the **Pilots** who hold it right now and its **Min. points**. Your own rank is marked, and a tip on a row says "The best 10.2% of your company hold Captain or better." A line under the table says who is on the ladder.
- **The list**: the best **50 pilots of your company across all three worlds**, with place, symbol, name and PvE points. Your own row is marked.

- A pilot with no PvE points yet is not on the ladder and not in the list: destroy an alien to get on it. A pilot who has been away for 30 days is not on it either, until he launches or opens the page again.
- Only names and public numbers are shown.

## Promotions

- When you reach a new best rank you get a toast ("Promoted: Senior Sergeant") and a short, quiet sound. "Best" means the best rank you have been told since you last launched: if you fall a grade and win it back, there is no second message. A rank you rose to while you were away is told once, at your next login. A fall is never told.
- From **Junior Major** up, a promotion also shows in the chat's **System** tab for the pilots of your company who are flying in your world: "Nova was promoted to Junior Major." The company hears it only when your own PvE points lifted you; if you rose because other pilots left the ladder, you get your toast and nobody else is told.

## Ranks and the wipe

A wipe changes nobody's points. It keeps your level, experience and kill statistics ([Wipe Timeline](/wiki/03-Mechanics/Wipe-Timeline.md#cross-season-progression-permanent-buffs-)), and your PvE points are worked out from them, so the ladder of your company is in the same order after the wipe. Your rank is no longer for life, though: it follows your place, so it moves as the pilots of your company do, and a pilot who stays away for 30 days leaves the ladder until he flies again.

## How long it takes

These are **estimates** from a model of how fast a pilot hunts, not measurements. The minimum of points is not the hard part: a pilot who hunts about two hours a day, 58 hours in a season, has about 21,200 PvE points after one season, more than even the Senior Admiral asks for. In hours of hunting, a Pilot's minimum comes inside the first hour, a Sergeant's in 4, a Captain's in about 9, a Major's in 14, a Colonel's in 22, a General's in 32, an Admiral's in 48 and the Senior Admiral's in 55.

What decides your rank is your place, and the other pilots hunt too. In a company of 31, only the best four pilots can be Captains or better, however many points the others have. The minimum of points stops a young company from handing out the top ranks; the place keeps the top of an old company small.
